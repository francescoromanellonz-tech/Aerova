The single biggest issue: the site shows the Aerova unit in isolation — on white, in renders, as a spinning sequence — but rarely in place. We own the product shot. We don't yet own the room.

The story Aerova sells is "pure water, tirée de l'air" — pulled from the air around you. That "around you" is missing. Every page needs at least one hero lifestyle frame where the machine is unmistakably Aerova, unmistakably in a beautiful real environment, and the water is the quiet subject.

06  ·  Visual direction

The bar every new asset must clear.

A short brief for the shoot. Pin this on the monitor — the rules are simple and the fewer of them the better.

✓Do

  • Real spaces, real light. Warm 3000–3400K. Natural where possible. Shadows welcome.
  • The machine is always the Aerova. Same finish, same silhouette, nothing swapped.
  • Water as the subject. The glass, the pour, the level. The unit is secondary.
  • Quiet compositions. Negative space around the product. Minimal styling.
  • Hands over faces. When people appear, fragments first — a hand on a glass, a shoulder passing.
  • Muted, editorial palette. Bone, warm greige, deep teal accent, matte black. No saturated blues.

✗Don't

  • No generic appliances. If it isn't clearly the Aerova unit, it can't be in frame.
  • No CGI rooms. No rendered lifestyle, no synthesised interiors, no AI-generated "atmosphere."
  • No cool fluorescent grade. Avoid the sterile showroom look the current lifestyles carry.
  • No stock families. No laughing groups at kitchen islands. We're not selling an appliance.
  • No floating product on gradient. The hero must land in a real place, not a void.
  • No text burned into imagery. Copy lives in HTML so we can localise.
