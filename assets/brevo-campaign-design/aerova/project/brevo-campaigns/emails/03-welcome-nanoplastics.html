You cannot see them. You cannot taste them. But they are there in every sip.

240,000. That is the number of plastic particles scientists have found in a single litre of premium bottled water.

More than 90% are nanoplastics. The word sounds technical, but what it means is simple: they are small enough to pass through the walls of your gut and enter your bloodstream. Researchers have found them in human lungs, kidneys, livers, and brains. They have been found in the placentas of unborn babies.

What does this actually mean for how you feel?

The research is still developing, but here is what scientists have already connected to long-term plastic-chemical exposure. Phthalates — the chemicals that make plastic flexible — have been linked to lower testosterone in men and hormonal disruption in women. Studies have found a connection between high plastic-chemical exposure and reduced sperm count. Some researchers believe it may be making it harder for couples to conceive.

The chemicals also act on the thyroid, which affects energy, metabolism, and mood. Many people with high plastic exposure report persistent fatigue that does not improve with rest.

In short: nanoplastics may be making you more tired, disrupting your hormones, and — for couples trying to have children — part of a problem usually blamed on stress or age.

Where do the plastics come from? The bottle itself. PET breaks down with heat, age, and pressure. Every twist of the cap generates roughly 500 particles. Transport, warm storage, and repeated opening make it worse.
