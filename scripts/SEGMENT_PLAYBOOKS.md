## Playbook 1: Villa Owners (Airbnb / Short-Term Rental)

### Persona in 3 Sentences
Vietnamese or expat property investor with 1–3 villas in District 2, District 7, or Thảo Điền. Running on Airbnb or Booking.com with 60–80% occupancy; Superhost status matters to them. They manage the property themselves or through a small local operator, and they make purchasing decisions alone — no owner committee, no board sign-off.

### Top 3 Pain Points
1. **Guest experience gaps** — They know a 7-bedroom private villa serving guests plastic-bottled water looks inconsistent. They've read reviews where guests mention water quality or sustainability. This prick of status anxiety is real.
2. **Logistics friction** — Weekly water deliveries, storage management, running out during peak occupancy. Every delivery is a coordination task they didn't sign up for.
3. **Rising delivery costs** — Bottled water pricing from distributors has increased 15–25% since 2023. They're paying more for the same problem.

### Top 3 Buying Triggers
1. Seeing a specific number ("3,500 bottles per month") that makes the status quo feel absurd
2. A local reference — another Superhost or villa operator in the same area has switched
3. The pilot offer — zero-risk 30-day trial removes the decision barrier entirely

### Hooks That Work
- "Your guests pay [X] per night — what do they drink?"
- The sustainability/review angle: "Guests notice the plastic"
- The logistics-free angle: "Never order water again"
- The Superhost status angle: "An amenity that moves you from good to exceptional"

### Subject Line Bank
1. `[Property name] — a question about guest water`
2. `What do your Airbnb guests drink when they're thirsty at 2am?`
3. `3,500 fewer plastic bottles per month at a villa in An Phu`
4. `Your [X]-bedroom villa — the one thing missing from a five-star experience`
5. `How a Thảo Điền Superhost eliminated their water delivery entirely`
6. `Re: AEROVA — the numbers for your property`
7. `$3.01/day for unlimited pure water at [Property name]`

## Playbook 2: Corporate Offices

### Persona in 3 Sentences
Vietnamese HR Director, Operations Manager, or Office Manager at a 30–200 person company in HCMC (District 1, Bình Thạnh, PMH). Mid-to-large company with international staff or aspirations; sustainability reporting is becoming relevant. They have a water cooler contract or regular bottled water delivery, and they are the budget-holder for facilities spend up to ~$2,000/month.

### Top 3 Pain Points
1. **Productivity tax** — Water cooler runs out, someone has to order, delivery is delayed, staff complain. This is a facilities problem that recurs monthly.
2. **ESG pressure** — Vietnamese companies above a certain size are beginning to report on sustainability for investor relations or multinational clients. Plastic water use is an easy-to-measure metric.
3. **Staff wellbeing signal** — The quality of office water is a visible signal of how much the company invests in its people. Premium water is a recruitment and retention conversation.

### Top 3 Buying Triggers
1. A concrete cost-per-employee comparison (they didn't know the numbers before you showed them)
2. ESG reporting context — a metric that appears in the sustainability section
3. A peer company in the same sector has already installed

### Hooks That Work
- The cost-per-head calculation (most haven't done this)
- The sustainability angle ("eliminate X bottles from your office footprint")
- The employee health angle ("your team drinks [alkaline / pure / mineralised] water")
- The facilities-simplification angle ("no more cooler contracts, no more delivery coordination")

### Subject Line Bank
1. `A question about what your [30 / 50 / 100] staff drink every day`
2. `The hidden water cost in your office — and how to eliminate it`

### Top 3 Pain Points
1. **Review differentiation** — In a market where Booking.com scores separate the 8.5 and 9.5 properties, every amenity detail matters. Water is often a review trigger.

3. **Plastic waste and ESG signal** — Eco-conscious travellers (a growing segment in Vietnam) leave reviews that mention plastic. Boutique hotels are more sensitive to this than chains.

## Playbook 4: Luxury Hotels (50+ rooms)

### Persona in 3 Sentences
Senior F&B Manager, Director of Rooms, or VP Operations at a 4–5 star property. Decision is made through a procurement committee; the GM can champion but cannot approve alone. Relationship and brand alignment matter — they will not install a machine they haven't vetted thoroughly.

### Top 3 Pain Points
1. **Brand alignment** — Can AEROVA match the aesthetic of their property? This question must be answered visually before any conversation about cost.
2. **Procurement process** — They want a formal proposal, references, certifications, and an installation record before they take it to procurement.
3. **Guest expectation gap** — Premium guests increasingly ask about water provenance and sustainability. Bottled water in luxury settings is beginning to feel antiquated.

### Top 3 Buying Triggers
1. A comparable luxury property (same star rating, similar positioning) has installed
2. Certifications: NSF/ANSI 42+58, WHO/QCVN compliance, 2-year warranty — these must be documented
3. The pilot offer: a department-level pilot (e.g., executive floor only) is a lower-risk entry point

### Subject Line Bank
1. `[Hotel name] — a water amenity your competitors haven't adopted yet`
2. `How [Comparable luxury property] is offering unlimited in-room water at no marginal cost`
3. `The water standard your premium guests are starting to expect`
4. `AEROVA for [Hotel name] — NSF/ANSI certified atmospheric water, produced on property`
5. `Re: A proposal for [Hotel name]'s executive floor water amenity`

### Notes for Luxury Segment
- Lead with certifications and the machine's aesthetic before anything else
- Use the hotel suite hero image (`product-hero-scene-2-desktop-v2.png`) in every HTML email
- Never mention price in Email 1 — position as a brand conversation first
- Offer a department pilot (executive floor, spa, or F&B) rather than full-property from day 1
- Prepare a formal PDF proposal for any property that engages; email alone will not close this segment

## Universal Rules (Apply to All Playbooks)

1. **Never use first names in cold Email 1** — "Dear Mr. Nguyen" not "Dear Minh"
2. **Always include the Vietnamese P.S. line** — it disproportionately increases open/reply rates
3. **Personalise Email 1 with one specific detail** — a listing URL, a review, a district, a property name
4. **The demo is the goal of Emails 1–4** — not a sale, not a decision, just a 15-minute visit
5. **Plain text for all cold emails** — HTML only enters the sequence after first engagement
6. **Zalo on Day 24** — not optional; this is the channel Vietnamese business happens on
7. **Give the GM copy to forward upward** — in Vietnamese-led businesses, the GM is rarely the final decision-maker; they need language they can forward to the owner
8. **Never discount** — reframe via daily rate, payback period, or pilot offer instead
9. **One CTA per email** — never give two options; pick the one action you want them to take
